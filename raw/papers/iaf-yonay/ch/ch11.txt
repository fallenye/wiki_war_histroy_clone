The Israeli Air Force, veterans often claim, was born in Ramat
David. This is true neither semantically nor historically. There were
Palestine’s early aviators, and the Haganah's underground gliding
and aviation clubs, and especially the Flight Platoon of the Pal-
mach. Then there were the air forces of the World War II allies,
whose veterans helped Israel win its independence, and the Czech
Air Force, which provided the planes for that victory and the
training of the first generation of Israeli military pilots.
  Yet in several key areas this old canard is true. It was at Ramat
David, the far-flung base in the Yizre’el Valley farm country, that
the lAF developed its Israeliness. It was there that its combat spirit
